# Template: External content (family `external`)

External content is the thing itself: ad copy, a blog post, social posts, a
proposal, contract redlines. Its body is written for the end audience in the
brand's or counsel's voice, not in pyramid form. The template wraps it so the
internal reviewer (brand QA, deal desk, counsel) can judge it without guessing
the intent.

## Sections (exact `##` headings, this order)
1. `## Brief`. Who this is for, where it runs, what it must achieve, in one to
   three sentences. Under 80 words, no lists.
2. `## Content`. The deliverable itself, verbatim as it would ship. Variants
   (A/B, per channel, per clause) as `###` sub-headings.
3. `## Notes for review`. What the reviewer must check: factual claims and
   their sources, brand or legal constraints applied, anything you could not
   resolve. "Nothing." when there is none.

## Example (illustrative; no marketing run has completed under this standard yet)

```
# Social copy: Brush Up launch week

## Brief
Three LinkedIn posts for founders and small-team leads, launch week of Brush
Up 2.0. Goal: 500 waitlist sign-ups; voice: direct, no exclamation marks.

## Content
### Post 1 (Mon)
Your team's docs are three tools deep. Brush Up 2.0 puts the one that matters
on the lock screen. Waitlist opens today. [link]
### Post 2 (Wed)
...
### Post 3 (Fri)
...

## Notes for review
- "three tools deep" is a claim from the 2026 survey in campaign-brief.md; keep or cut together.
- No pricing mentioned per the brief; confirm before Friday's post.
```
